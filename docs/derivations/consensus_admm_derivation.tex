\begin{abstract}
We state the coupled multi-agent MPC problem for $N$ double integrators coupled by formation
costs, split it by introducing local copies of neighbour trajectories, and derive the
resulting consensus ADMM iteration. The $z$-update collapses to a neighbourhood average, so
the method needs only one round of neighbour communication per iteration. The final
algorithm block is the specification the Python and C++ implementations follow line for
line.
\end{abstract}

\section{The coupled problem}

There are $N$ agents, each a discrete double integrator (Section 2 of the notation
reference). Agent $i$ has state $x_i \in \R^n$ and input $u_i \in \R^m$ with $n = 2d$ and
$m = d$. Over the horizon the condensed position prediction is
\begin{equation}
p_i = \Phi_p\, x_i(0) + \Gamma_p\, U_i,
\qquad
U_i = [u_i(0);\ \dots;\ u_i(T-1)] \in \R^{Tm}.
\label{eq:condensed}
\end{equation}
The centralised (open-loop) MPC problem is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{U_1,\dots,U_N} \quad
\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^T &\Big[
  q_p \norm{p_i(t) - p_i^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)}^2
+ q_v \norm{v_i(t)}^2
+ r \norm{u_i(t)}^2 \\
&\qquad + p_T \norm{p_i(T) - p_i^{\mathrm{ref}}(T)}^2 \Big]
+ w_f \sum_{(i,j)\in\Eset} \sum_{t=1}^T \norm{(p_i(t) - p_j(t)) - d_{ij}}^2
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:coupled}
\end{equation}
subject to, for every $i$ and $t$,
\begin{equation}
x_i(t+1) = A x_i(t) + B u_i(t),
\qquad
|u_i(t)| \le u_{\max},
\qquad
|v_i(t)| \le v_{\max},
\qquad
p_{\min} \le p_i(t) \le p_{\max}.
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:condensed} into \eqref{eq:coupled} gives a single convex QP in the
$N T d$ input variables: a block-diagonal Hessian (tracking, velocity, effort) plus a
formation coupling whose support is exactly $\Gset$. For $N = 8$, $T = 20$, $d = 2$ this is
a $320$-variable QP with $\mathcal O(NTd + |\Eset| T d)$ Hessian nonzeros --- small enough
to solve centrally, but this repo targets deployments with no central solver.

\section{Where the coupling lives}

Every term in \eqref{eq:coupled} except one is already separable across agents: the
tracking, velocity, effort and terminal costs each involve a single $i$, and the box
constraints are per-agent. The only inseparable terms are the formation edge costs
$\norm{(p_i - p_j) - d_{ij}}^2$, which contain two distinct agents' positions. Making this
explicit tells us exactly which variables need copying: we must copy $p_j$ for every edge
$(i,j)$ incident to $i$, i.e. for every $j \in \Nbr_i$.

\section{Local copies}

Introduce, for each agent $i$ and each $j \in \Nbrc_i$, a local copy $\ycopy{i}{j} \in
\R^{Td}$ of agent $j$'s position trajectory, and a global consensus trajectory
$\zcon{j} \in \R^{Td}$. The copy-based problem is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{U_i, \ycopy{i}{j}\}} \sum_i f_i\big(\ycopy{i}{i}, \{\ycopy{i}{j}\}_{j\in\Nbr_i}\big)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\ycopy{i}{j} = \zcon{j} \ \ \forall\, i,\ j\in\Nbrc_i,
\qquad
\ycopy{i}{i} = \Phi_p x_i(0) + \Gamma_p U_i,
\label{eq:copybased}
\end{equation}
where $f_i$ collects agent $i$'s tracking, effort and incident formation costs.

\begin{lemma}[equivalence]
\label{lem:equiv}
Problems \eqref{eq:coupled} and \eqref{eq:copybased} have the same optimal objective value,
and their optimisers are in one-to-one correspondence.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At any feasible point of \eqref{eq:copybased} the consensus constraints force
$\ycopy{i}{j} = \zcon{j}$ for every copy, so all copies of $j$ collapse to a single
trajectory and the objective reproduces \eqref{eq:coupled}. Conversely, any solution of
\eqref{eq:coupled} defines $\zcon{j} = p_j$ and $\ycopy{i}{j} = p_j$, which is feasible for
\eqref{eq:copybased} with the same objective. The two maps are inverses.
\end{proof}

This lemma is immediate, but it is the step that licenses everything else: it replaces a
coupled QP by a separable problem plus consensus constraints.

\section{Augmented Lagrangian and the three updates}

With unscaled multipliers $\udual{i}{j}$ on each consensus constraint, the augmented
Lagrangian of \eqref{eq:copybased} is
\begin{equation}
\Lrho = \sum_i f_i(y_i) + \sum_{i}\sum_{j\in\Nbrc_i}\Big[
  \udual{i}{j}\cdot(\ycopy{i}{j} - \zcon{j})
  + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\norm{\ycopy{i}{j} - \zcon{j}}^2 \Big],
\label{eq:auglagmulti}
\end{equation}
where $y_i$ abbreviates the tuple $(\ycopy{i}{i}, \{\ycopy{i}{j}\}_{j\in\Nbr_i})$. ADMM
alternates three minimisations, each holding the other two blocks fixed.

\paragraph{x-update.} Minimise over $\{U_i, \ycopy{i}{j}\}$ for every $i$ independently.
Both $f_i$ and the penalty separate across $i$, so this is $N$ independent QPs:
\begin{equation}
(U_i^{k+1}, y_i^{k+1}) = \argmin_{U_i, y_i}\ \Big[
  f_i(y_i) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\sum_{j\in\Nbrc_i}\norm{\ycopy{i}{j} - \zconk{j}{k} + \dualk{i}{j}{k}}^2
\Big]
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \ycopy{i}{i} = \Phi_p x_i(0) + \Gamma_p U_i,\ \text{boxes}.
\label{eq:xupdate}
\end{equation}
(The scaled dual is already in use; see Section 5.) Only $\ycopy{i}{i}$ is dynamically
constrained; the copies $\ycopy{i}{j}$ for $j \ne i$ are free and pinned only by the
consensus penalty. This is exactly \texttt{CvxpyAgentSolver}.

\paragraph{z-update.} Minimise over $\zcon{j}$ for every $j$ independently. Setting
$\grad_{\zcon{j}}\Lrho = 0$ gives
\begin{equation}
0 = \sum_{i : j \in \Nbrc_i}\Big[ -\udual{i}{j} - \rho(\ycopy{i}{j} - \zcon{j}) \Big],
\end{equation}
and in the scaled dual $\dual{i}{j} = \udual{i}{j}/\rho$ this is
\begin{equation}
\zcon{j} = \frac{1}{|C(j)|}\sum_{i \in C(j)} \big(\ycopy{i}{j} + \dual{i}{j}\big),
\qquad C(j) = \{ i : j \in \Nbrc_i \},
\label{eq:zupdate}
\end{equation}
the average of $\ycopy{i}{j} + \dual{i}{j}$ over the contributors $C(j)$ of $j$. For an
undirected graph $C(j) = \Nbrc_j$, so \textbf{the $z$-update for $j$ requires only messages
from $j$'s neighbours}. This single sentence is what makes the iteration distributed.

\paragraph{Dual update.} $\udualk{i}{j}{k+1} = \udualk{i}{j}{k} + \rho(\ycopyk{i}{j}{k+1} -
\zconk{j}{k+1})$, or in scaled form
$\dualk{i}{j}{k+1} = \dualk{i}{j}{k} + (\ycopyk{i}{j}{k+1} - \zconk{j}{k+1})$.

\section{Scaled form and the final algorithm}

The substitution $\dual{i}{j} = \udual{i}{j}/\rho$ absorbs $\rho$ into the penalty and
reduces the dual update to residual accumulation (Section 6 of
\texttt{augmented\_lagrangian.tex}). The full iteration, in the form the code implements, is

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Consensus ADMM for distributed MPC (scaled dual)}
\label{alg:admm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{input:} $x_i(0)$, references, formation offsets $d_{ij}$, $\rho > 0$, $\alpha \in (0,2)$
\State \textbf{init:} $\ycopy{i}{j} \gets 0$, $\dual{i}{j} \gets 0$, $\zcon{j} \gets 0$
\For{$k = 0, 1, \dots$}
  \State \textbf{x-update (parallel):} solve \eqref{eq:xupdate} for every $i$
  \State \textbf{relax:} $\hat{y}_i^j \gets \alpha\,\ycopy{i}{j} + (1-\alpha)\,\zcon{j}$
  \State \textbf{z-update:} $\zcon{j} \gets \tfrac{1}{|C(j)|}\sum_{i\in C(j)}(\hat{y}_i^j + \dual{i}{j})$
  \State \textbf{broadcast} $\zcon{j}$ to all $i \in C(j)$
  \State \textbf{dual update:} $\dual{i}{j} \gets \dual{i}{j} + (\hat{y}_i^j - \zcon{j})$
  \State \textbf{stop} when $r^k \le \varepsilon^{\mathrm{pri}}$ and $s^k \le \varepsilon^{\mathrm{dual}}$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Each line maps one-to-one onto \texttt{ConsensusADMM}: the x-update is \verb|_x_update|,
the relaxation \verb|_relax|, the z-update \verb|_z_update|, the broadcast
\verb|_broadcast_consensus| and the dual update \verb|_dual_update|.

\section{Over-relaxation}

The relaxation $\hat{y}_i^j = \alpha\,\ycopy{i}{j} + (1-\alpha)\,\zcon{j}$ leaves the fixed
points of the iteration unchanged (at a fixed point $\ycopy{i}{j} = \zcon{j}$, so
$\hat{y}_i^j = \zcon{j}$) but changes the transient. The admissible range is
$\alpha \in (0, 2)$: $\alpha = 1$ is plain ADMM, $\alpha \ge 2$ diverges, and the practical
choice is $1.5$--$1.8$, which typically halves the iteration count. The relaxed quantity
$\hat{y}_i^j$ (not $\ycopy{i}{j}$) feeds the z-update and the dual update, matching
\verb|_relax|.

\section{Residuals and stopping}

The primal and dual residuals are (Section 6 of the notation reference)
\begin{equation}
r^k = \Big(\sum_i\sum_{j\in\Nbrc_i}\norm{\ycopy{i}{j} - \zcon{j}}^2\Big)^{1/2},
\qquad
s^k = \rho\Big(\sum_j \norm{\zconk{j}{k} - \zconk{j}{k-1}}^2\Big)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
and stopping uses
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{pri}} = \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{dual}}}\,\varepsilon^{\mathrm{abs}}
+ \varepsilon^{\mathrm{rel}}\max\{\norm{y}, \norm{z}\},
\qquad
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{dual}} = \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{dual}}}\,\varepsilon^{\mathrm{abs}}
+ \varepsilon^{\mathrm{rel}}\,\rho\,\norm{\lambda},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{dual}} = \sum_i |\Nbrc_i|\, T\, d
\end{equation}
counts the scalar consensus constraints: each $(i,j)$ with $j \in \Nbrc_i$ contributes a
$Td$-vector, hence $|\Nbrc_i|\, T d$ scalars per agent. Convergence is declared when both
residuals are within tolerance.

\section{Communication and computation cost}

Per iteration, agent $i$ solves one QP with $(1 + |\Nbrc_i|)\,Td$ scalar trajectory
variables (its own trajectory plus one copy per closed-neighbour) plus $Tm$ inputs. In the
z-update it exchanges $2|\Nbr_i|$ messages of $Td$ floats --- one $(\hat{y}_i^j +
\dual{i}{j})$ push and one $\zcon{j}$ broadcast per neighbour. Contrast with the
centralised QP of Section 1, which has $NTd$ variables coupled through the formation
Hessian: the per-agent subproblem is a factor $N/(1+|\Nbrc_i|)$ smaller and, critically,
requires no global data structure.

\section{Receding horizon}

In closed loop the solver runs once per control step with the measured state $x_i(0)$, then
applies the first input $u_i(0)$ and shifts the previous iterate as the warm start
(\verb|ADMMResult.shifted|). The warm start matters: the previous consensus is typically
already within the new problem's basin, so a handful of warm-started iterations replaces a
full cold solve.

An honest statement about feasibility and stability: the box constraints make every local
QP feasible at every step, so the algorithm never stalls for lack of a feasible point. But
no terminal set or terminal cost is imposed, so recursive feasibility and closed-loop
stability are \emph{not guaranteed} by the standard MPC theory; they are demonstrated
numerically in the notebooks. State this plainly --- an advisor will check for exactly this
claim.

\section{Worked example}

Set $d = 1$, $\Delta t = 1$, $T = 2$, so the state is $x = [p,\,v]^\top$ and
\begin{equation}
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The condensed position map $y = \Phi_p x(0) + \Gamma_p U$ has
\begin{equation}
\Phi_p = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\Gamma_p = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0 \\ 1.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
(Block row $r$ of $\Phi_p$ is $A^{r+1}$'s position row, and block $(r,s)$ of $\Gamma_p$ is
the position row of $A^{r-s}B$.)

To keep the arithmetic transparent we illustrate the ADMM mechanics on the pure-consensus
core of the problem, dropping the dynamics: two agents each hold a scalar copy $y_i$ of a
common value, with local cost $\tfrac12 (y_i - a_i)^2$ and targets $a_1 = 1$, $a_2 = 3$.
The true optimum is the average $y_1 = y_2 = z = 2$. With $\rho = 1$ the scaled iteration
is
\begin{equation}
y_i^{k+1} = \argmin_{y_i}\ \tfrac12(y_i - a_i)^2 + \tfrac12(y_i - z^k + \lambda_i^k)^2
= \frac{a_i + z^k - \lambda_i^k}{2},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
z^{k+1} = \tfrac12\big[(y_1^{k+1} + \lambda_1^k) + (y_2^{k+1} + \lambda_2^k)\big],
\qquad
\lambda_i^{k+1} = \lambda_i^k + (y_i^{k+1} - z^{k+1}).
\end{equation}

Starting from $z^0 = 0$, $\lambda_1^0 = \lambda_2^0 = 0$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cc|cc}
$k$ & $y_1^k$ & $y_2^k$ & $z^k$ & $(\lambda_1^k, \lambda_2^k)$ \\
\hline
$0$ & $0.50$ & $1.50$ & $1.00$ & $(-0.50, +0.50)$ \\
$1$ & $1.25$ & $1.75$ & $1.50$ & $(-0.75, +0.75)$ \\
$2$ & $1.625$ & $1.875$ & $1.75$ & $(-0.875, +0.875)$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The copies converge to $2$ and the duals to $(-1, +1)$, which is exactly the dual
feasibility condition $(y_i - a_i) + \lambda_i = 0$ at the optimum. The MPC layer only
adds the affine map $y = \Phi_p x_0 + \Gamma_p U$ to the x-update; the z-update and dual
update above are unchanged.
