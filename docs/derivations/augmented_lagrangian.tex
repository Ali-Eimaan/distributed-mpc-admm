\begin{abstract}
The augmented Lagrangian adds a quadratic penalty $\tfrac{\rho}{2}\norm{Ax-b}_2^2$ to the
ordinary Lagrangian. The penalty leaves the solution set unchanged but makes the dual
function differentiable with a known $1/\rho$-Lipschitz gradient, so it converges by
gradient ascent even when $f$ is merely convex.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Consider the equality-constrained convex program
\begin{equation}
\min_x \; f(x) \qquad\text{subject to}\qquad Ax = b,
\label{eq:problem}
\end{equation}
with $f : \R^n \to \R \cup \{+\infty\}$ closed, proper and convex, $A \in \R^{m\times n}$
and $b \in \R^m$. Two standing assumptions are used throughout:

\begin{assumption}[closed, proper, convex]
\label{ass:cpc}
$f$ is closed, proper and convex.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[saddle point]
\label{ass:saddle}
The ordinary Lagrangian $L(x,\nu) = f(x) + \nu^\top(Ax-b)$ has a saddle point;
equivalently, strong duality holds and a primal--dual optimal pair $(x^\star,\nu^\star)$
exists.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:cpc} makes the augmented problem of Section~\ref{sec:augmented}
well-posed; Assumption~\ref{ass:saddle} licenses the dual step of
Section~\ref{sec:multipliers}. We deliberately do \emph{not} assume strict convexity of
$f$: that omission is the entire point of the augmented Lagrangian.

\section{From the Lagrangian to dual ascent}
\label{sec:dualascent}

The ordinary Lagrangian and the dual function are
\begin{equation}
L(x,\nu) = f(x) + \nu^\top(Ax-b),
\qquad
g(\nu) = \inf_x L(x,\nu).
\end{equation}
The dual problem $\max_\nu g(\nu)$ is solved by (sub)gradient ascent. At a multiplier
$\nu^k$, a subgradient of $g$ is the constraint residual at the minimiser:
\begin{equation}
x^{k+1} \in \argmin_x L(x, \nu^k),
\qquad
\nu^{k+1} = \nu^k + \alpha^k\,(A x^{k+1} - b),
\label{eq:dualascent}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha^k > 0$ is a step size. Two failure modes make this unusable in practice.

First, when $f$ is merely convex the inner problem
$\inf_x L(x,\nu^k) = \inf_x \big[ f(x) + \nu^k{}^\top A x \big]$ need not have a unique
minimiser and can be unbounded below, so $x^{k+1}$ is ill-defined or multi-valued. This is
exactly the rendezvous case of the MPC problem in this repository: with $q_v = r = 0$ the
follower copies are pinned only by a linear dual term.

Second, even when $x^{k+1}$ exists, the step-size sequence $\alpha^k$ must be tuned to an
unknown Lipschitz constant. The dual of a merely convex primal is non-smooth, so a constant
step is not guaranteed to converge while a diminishing step is slow; neither constant is
available a priori.

\section{The augmented Lagrangian}
\label{sec:augmented}

Add a quadratic penalty to the ordinary Lagrangian:
\begin{equation}
L_\rho(x,\nu) = f(x) + \nu^\top(Ax-b) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\norm{Ax-b}_2^2,
\qquad \rho > 0.
\label{eq:auglag}
\end{equation}
The added term does not change the solution set: on the feasible set $Ax = b$ the penalty
vanishes and $L_\rho = L$, while off the feasible set it only increases the cost, so any
minimiser of \eqref{eq:auglag} subject to $Ax=b$ is a minimiser of \eqref{eq:problem} and
vice versa. The important effect is on the \emph{dual}. The augmented dual function
\begin{equation}
g_\rho(\nu) = \min_x L_\rho(x,\nu)
\end{equation}
is differentiable with gradient
\begin{equation}
\nabla g_\rho(\nu) = A x^\star_\rho(\nu) - b,
\qquad
x^\star_\rho(\nu) = \argmin_x L_\rho(x,\nu),
\label{eq:danskin}
\end{equation}
and this gradient is $1/\rho$-Lipschitz. The differentiability is a Danskin-type argument:
the quadratic penalty makes the minimiser unique even when $f$ is merely convex, so the
envelope theorem applies. The Lipschitz constant $1/\rho$ is \emph{known} --- it is the
penalty parameter itself --- which is exactly what dual ascent lacked. One added quadratic
term converts a non-smooth dual into a smooth one whose smoothness constant we choose; this
is the whole reason the method works and it deserves the emphasis, not a citation.

\section{Method of multipliers}
\label{sec:multipliers}

The method of multipliers alternates the primal minimisation with a dual step whose size is
exactly the penalty parameter:
\begin{equation}
x^{k+1} = \argmin_x L_\rho(x, \nu^k),
\qquad
\nu^{k+1} = \nu^k + \rho\,(A x^{k+1} - b).
\label{eq:mom}
\end{equation}
The step size $\rho$ is not a free choice layered on top of the penalty: they are the
\emph{same} parameter. To see why, write the optimality condition of the $x$-update,
\begin{equation}
0 = \nabla f(x^{k+1}) + A^\top \nu^k + \rho A^\top (A x^{k+1} - b)
  = \nabla f(x^{k+1}) + A^\top\!\big[\nu^k + \rho (A x^{k+1} - b)\big],
\end{equation}
and compare it with the stationarity condition at an optimum,
$\nabla f(x^\star) + A^\top \nu^\star = 0$. The bracket is exactly $\nu^{k+1}$; choosing
any step other than $\rho$ leaves a stationarity residual that the next $x$-update must
re-absorb. Equivalently, since $\nabla g_\rho$ is $1/\rho$-Lipschitz by \eqref{eq:danskin},
gradient ascent with the largest safe constant step is
$\nu^{k+1} = \nu^k + \rho\,\nabla g_\rho(\nu^k)$. This self-tuning property --- one
parameter playing both roles --- is what makes the method practical.

\section{Why the method of multipliers does not split}

Even when $f$ is separable, say $f(x) = \sum_i f_i(x_i)$ with $x = (x_1,\dots,x_p)$, the
$x$-update of \eqref{eq:mom} does not decompose, because the penalty
\begin{equation}
\tfrac{\rho}{2}\norm{Ax-b}_2^2
= \tfrac{\rho}{2}\Big\lVert \textstyle\sum_i A_i x_i - b \Big\rVert_2^2
\end{equation}
expands into cross terms $x_i^\top A_i^\top A_j x_j$ for every pair $(i,j)$ of blocks. The
joint minimisation over $x$ couples all blocks through $A$, so the method of multipliers is
a \emph{centralised} algorithm even for separable $f$. This is the precise gap that ADMM
closes: by introducing a local copy of the coupling variable and alternating over one block
at a time, each subproblem keeps the separability that the method of multipliers destroys.
That construction is the subject of the next document.

\section{Scaled dual form}

Complete the square in the multiplier and penalty terms of \eqref{eq:auglag}. Writing
$r = Ax - b$,
\begin{equation}
\nu^\top r + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\norm{r}^2
= \tfrac{\rho}{2}\norm{r + \nu/\rho}^2 - \tfrac{1}{2\rho}\norm{\nu}^2.
\end{equation}
Dropping the $\nu$-only constant (it does not affect the $x$-update) and substituting the
scaled dual $\lambda = \nu/\rho$ gives
\begin{equation}
L_\rho(x,\lambda) = f(x) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\norm{Ax - b + \lambda}^2,
\qquad
\lambda^{k+1} = \lambda^k + (A x^{k+1} - b).
\label{eq:scaled}
\end{equation}
The dual update is now the tidy residual accumulation $\lambda \leftarrow \lambda + r$. The
code in this repository stores the \emph{scaled} dual. A direct consequence: \textbf{any
change to $\rho$ must rescale $\lambda$ by the reciprocal factor},
$\lambda \leftarrow \lambda \cdot \rho_{\mathrm{old}}/\rho_{\mathrm{new}}$. This is the
single most common bug in adaptive-$\rho$ implementations, and it is handled explicitly in
\verb|ConsensusADMM._update_rho|.

\section{Interpretation}

Two readings, both short. First, \emph{proximal point}: the multiplier update of
\eqref{eq:mom} is a proximal step on the negated dual objective, so the method of
multipliers is the proximal point algorithm applied to the dual; the augmented term is the
Moreau regularisation that makes the prox map well-defined. Second, \emph{primal--dual
interpolation}: as $\rho \to 0$ the algorithm limits to dual ascent (smooth dual, slow and
step-size-sensitive), and as $\rho \to \infty$ it limits to the penalty method (fast primal
progress, ill-conditioned dual). Finite $\rho$ trades the two, which is why the
residual-balancing heuristic of the main document chooses $\rho$ online.
